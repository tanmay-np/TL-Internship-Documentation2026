⚙️ Core Topics

Edge Computing

Processing data near the source device (sensors, cameras, etc.) instead of sending everything to the cloud. This enables real‑time decision making in robotics and autonomous systems.

Sensors → Edge Device (local processing) → Decision → Actuator

✔ Low latency  |  ✔ Faster response  |  ✔ Better privacy  |  ✔ Reduced bandwidth

Microcontroller vs Microprocessor

Microcontroller
Built-in RAM, I/O, low power, embedded applications (Arduino, ESP32)
Microprocessor (MPU)
High computing power, external RAM/ROM, runs OS (computers, smartphones)

🔵 Active Sensors

Require external power, emit energy. Examples: Ultrasonic (distance), Radar (object detection), IR (proximity), LiDAR (3D mapping).
Applications: Robotics navigation, autonomous vehicles, obstacle avoidance.

🟢 Passive Sensors

No external excitation, detect natural energy. Examples: LDR (light), Thermistor (temperature), Photodiode (light sensing), Piezoelectric (vibration).
Applications: Environmental monitoring, energy harvesting, safety systems.

Control Systems (P, PI, PID)

P – Proportional (reduces error)
PI – Proportional + Integral (steady‑state accuracy)
PID – Adds Derivative (predictive, smooth response)

Widely used in robotics, drones, industrial automation.

Actuators (Electrical → Motion)

DC Motor – Simple speed control; used in robots, fans, drills. AC Motor – High power industrial; conveyors, pumps. Servo Motor – Precise position control; robotic arms, RC vehicles. Stepper Motor – Accurate step‑by‑step rotation; 3D printers, CNC. BLDC Motor – Efficient, long life; drones, electric vehicles. Harmonic Drive – High torque, zero backlash; space robotics, medical devices.

Servo motors give precise position control — essential for robotic arms.

Arduino vs ESP32

Arduino Uno/Nano/Mega
ATmega328P, beginner‑friendly, huge community. Ideal for sensor interfacing.
  • 5V logic, 16 MHz clock
  • Limited RAM (2KB) and Flash (32KB)
  • No built‑in wireless
  • Perfect for basic embedded projects
ESP32
Dual core, Wi‑Fi, BLE, high processing power — perfect for IoT and edge AI.
  • 3.3V logic, up to 240 MHz
  • 520KB RAM, 4MB Flash
  • Integrated Wi‑Fi + Bluetooth Low Energy
  • Great for real‑time cloud/edge applications

MOSFET

Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect Transistor — high‑speed switching device used in motor drivers, power supplies, and embedded electronics. Essential for PWM control of actuators.

📄 Microcontroller Datasheets

Arduino Uno (ATmega328P)

Microcontroller: ATmega328P
Operating Voltage: 5V
Input Voltage: 7-12V
Digital I/O Pins: 14 (6 PWM)
Analog Inputs: 6
Clock Speed: 16 MHz
Flash: 32 KB, SRAM: 2 KB, EEPROM: 1 KB
Protocols: UART, I2C, SPI

ESP32

CPU: Xtensa LX6 dual core
Clock Speed: up to 240 MHz
Wi-Fi: 802.11 b/g/n
Bluetooth: BLE + Classic
GPIO Pins: 34 (multipurpose)
ADC: 12‑bit, 18 channels
RAM: 520 KB SRAM, Flash: 4 MB
Protocols: I2C, SPI, UART, CAN
